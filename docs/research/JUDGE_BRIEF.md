# Pocket Glimmer: judge brief (one page)

> Supporting judge notes. Use docs/FINAL_HOURS.md for the current form draft and docs/PROJECT_STATUS.md for verification status. Originally written Nov 4 2026 ~6 AM ET. Every number below is in LEDGER.md §6 or eval/results/*.md (AFTER_FLOOR, PER_TYPE_TAU090, RISK_COVERAGE, SPARK_TEACHER, DRIFT_CHECK).
> `[PHONE_*]` = not measured on the iPhone yet. Fill it from the device run, or cut the line.

**1. Pitch.** A 1B Meta model built for iPhone that makes an assistant's everyday decisions offline, in one forward pass, and knows when it doesn't know. When it isn't sure, it hands the question to Muse Spark, with personal details masked first.

**2. Who it's for, and the moment.** I'm Habib, a CS student at Hunter College, and I live in the Bronx. My commute is underground with no signal, and the assistants I can use either forget me or keep my life on a server. Pocket Glimmer keeps its memory of me (facts, people, corrections) in SQLite on the phone, and it keeps working in airplane mode: reminders, calendar, contacts, recall, "text Mom I'll be home by 8". Meta's Glimmer launch post makes the same point: "An agent that manages your schedule, drafts your messages, organizes your files, and learns how you work needs deep access to personal context" (Meta, Aug 10 2026). Glimmer's ~4-bit build targets a 24–32 GB machine. This project moves its everyday judgment onto a phone.

**3. Main Meta model roles.**
| Model | Role | Access |
|---|---|---|
| **Llama 3.2 1B Instruct → Llama-Reflex-1B** | The phone model: 12 typed decisions, Q4_K_M GGUF (808 MB), llama.cpp + Metal | Llama 3.2 Community License, "Built with Llama" |
| **Muse Glimmer 30B** | The only teacher of the shipping model (run locally at IQ2_M; its option-letter probabilities are the soft labels; 892 labels; QC 43/50 against hand labels) | Apache-2.0 open weights |
| **Muse Spark 1.3** | Cloud escalation when confidence < τ, behind a privacy scrubber | Meta Model API, Standard mode only |
| **Muse Spark Open (816B-A42B)** | Tested as a stronger teacher via Baseten. **Rejected** on cal180: its labels matched the hand-written gold labels 178/180, but the 1B it taught was overconfident (accuracy on handled 0.90 vs 0.96, bound 13.7% vs 10.2%). Spend $11.06 | Muse Model License |

**4. Results.** Report-only split: 120 hand-labeled decisions, 10 per type. No shipping choice used it; 13 reporting runs are saved. One item = 0.83 percentage points. All results below are CPU decision measurements, not phone task-success measurements.
- Accuracy **0.74 → 0.82** (98/120)
- Decisions kept on-device at confidence ≥ 0.8: **15% → 51%** (61/120), 92% correct (56/61)
- Calibration error (ECE, 10 bins, raw letter softmax): **0.150 → 0.064**. Distillation produced this. No temperature scaling is used.
- **Shipping threshold τ = 0.90** (the app's default): 37% of test decisions stay on-device (44/120), 41/44 correct. Area under the risk–coverage curve 0.148 → 0.085 (eval/results/RISK_COVERAGE.md), so the gain isn't tied to one threshold.
- **Statistical limitation:** cal180 had 64/180 accepted and no errors. The nominal 10.2% calculation is not a post-selection certificate: those same examples also selected the model. The hand-authored, stratified set does not establish a deployment guarantee. The 5% target was not certified. [Evaluation correction](../EVALUATION_CLAIMS.md).

**5. Why this goes past a basic integration.**
- **Soft-label distillation from Glimmer's logits.** For each of 892 scenarios, Glimmer (llama-server) returns probabilities over the answer letters. Low-confidence labels were dropped (1,006 → 892). The student's LoRA (r16, 1 epoch) is trained only at the answer-letter position, with loss = soft-target cross-entropy against the teacher's letter distribution (KL up to a constant) + 0.5 × CE on the hard label.
- **One forward pass per decision.** Nothing is generated. The phone model reads the option-letter logits, applies a softmax, and compares the top probability to τ. Every decision type has a designated fallback option below τ (e.g. escalate, ask, confirm, keep both, skip).
- **Confidence-gated decisions.** The shipping threshold is 0.90. It was chosen using a nominal calculation on cal180; the measured accepted-decision counts are reported separately from any unsupported guarantee.
- **A closed loop.** The phone logs every decision (type, confidence, escalated, latency, later teacher agreement) and exports it as CSV. Those rows are the input for the next distillation round. One round has shipped so far.
- **Negative results kept.** 2 epochs (bound 14.2%), a nudge relabel (10.9%) and the Spark Open teacher (13.7%) all lost to the shipping model on cal180. Q8_0 and f16 don't beat Q4_K_M on cal180. Glimmer 30B can't run on the phone: IQ2_M is 9.85 GB, and Q1_0 outputs gibberish. A drift check found no damage to general ability (mean KL 0.0096 nats; 23/23 checkable answers).

**6. Best Agentic Use-Case.** Each request is a chain of typed decisions, and every link can stop safely: **route → toolChoice → contactPick/eventPick → confirmBeforeAct → act** (EventKit, Contacts, Shortcuts bridge). Messages always ask for confirmation. When contactPick is unsure (two "Sam"s in Contacts), the phone asks the user “Which Sam?” instead of choosing a contact. **Proactive:** iOS Shortcuts triggers (time, arrive, leave, email, message, charger) → App Intent → rules pick candidate memories → `nudgeNow` decides whether to interrupt. **Memory:** `rememberOrNot` and `memoryConflict` (update / keep both / ignore) run on device with provenance, and user corrections become retrievable memories. **Privacy:** when route says "escalate", a `sensitivity` decision can keep a health question on the phone ("Kept on device · nothing sent").

**7. Limitations.** All quality and calibration numbers come from a 300-item hand-labeled set (180 calibration / 120 test) written by one person, so per-type numbers (n=10) are noisy and real-traffic accuracy is unknown. Any future risk guarantee needs a new independent evaluation after model selection. `nudgeNow` is weak (3/10). It never clears τ, so it always takes its safe fallback. `memoryConflict` (9/10) and `confirmBeforeAct` (10/10) are accurate but rarely clear τ = 0.90 (0/10 and 2/10), so messages are confirmed by a hard rule, not by confidence. Every latency and RAM figure so far was measured on an x86 Linux CPU, not the iPhone. Current iPhone speed, memory, and thermal behavior are unmeasured. Tool arguments are filled by on-device heuristics and memory, not grammar-constrained decoding. The scrubber is regex- and contact-based and will miss some identifiers. We have not found prior art for this exact combination on a phone (the student's own letter-logit confidence, a Learn-Then-Test threshold, offline operation and a privacy veto together); certified routing thresholds on their own exist (Uddin & Bauer, ACL 2026 SRW; Conformal Cascade; CR²), see docs/research/RELATED_WORK.md.
